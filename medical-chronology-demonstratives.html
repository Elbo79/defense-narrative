Medical chronology demonstratives: turning the record into an argument the audience can see.

A medical chronology becomes persuasive when it reveals relationships in the evidence—not when it simply reproduces every appointment.

Medical records are chronological by nature but rarely clear as a narrative. The defense opportunity is to identify the timing that matters and make those relationships visible.

What a useful chronology can reveal

Depending on the evidence, a defense chronology may help an audience see pre-existing complaints, treatment before the event, gaps in care, delayed reporting, changing symptom descriptions, intervening events, diagnostic timing, periods of improvement, or the relationship between objective findings and claimed limitations.

Do not turn the demonstrative into another medical record

Showing every visit often recreates the same problem the visual was supposed to solve. A demonstrative should prioritize events tied to the disputed issue and allow supporting detail to be introduced when necessary.

Design around the argument

If the issue is causation, emphasize timing and competing causes. If the issue is future damages, show treatment trajectory and the basis for projections. If credibility matters, align statements, records, testimony, and objective events on the same visual framework.

Build it so counsel can use it

A chronology should work at mediation, during expert examination, in opening or closing when permitted, and as a roadmap for the trial team. The underlying source references should remain organized so counsel can move from the visual to the evidence supporting it.
