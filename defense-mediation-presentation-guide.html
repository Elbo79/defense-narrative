Mediation strategy guide

How to build a defense mediation presentation that changes the conversation.

A mediation presentation should not be the brief on slides. Its job is to make the defense view of exposure easier to see, evaluate, and remember.

The strongest presentation is selective. It concentrates attention on the facts that should change the other side’s assessment of trial risk.

Start with the decision, not the chronology

Ask what the mediator, carrier, client, or opposing side needs to understand differently when the presentation ends. That may be a causation gap, a credibility problem, an alternative liability explanation, an unsupported future-damages assumption, or a meaningful risk on both sides.

Build around four questions

What actually happened?

Use the record to establish a clear competing account of the event rather than responding point-by-point to the plaintiff narrative.

What caused the claimed harm?

Medical chronologies, prior conditions, treatment gaps, mechanism evidence, and expert opinions can help separate temporal sequence from legal or medical causation.

What are the damages assumptions?

Break future claims into understandable components. Visual comparisons can expose assumptions in life-care plans, wage claims, medical projections, or economic models without burying the audience in spreadsheets.

What happens if the case is tried?

Mediation is a decision environment. The presentation should help the room evaluate uncertainty, credibility, evidentiary disputes, and the practical consequences of taking the case to verdict.

Use visuals where they change comprehension

A timeline should clarify timing. A comparison should expose a difference. A demonstrative should simplify something difficult. Avoid decorating slides merely because the information can be visualized. See our approach to defense litigation graphics and demonstratives.

Finish with a coherent defense narrative

The audience should leave able to explain the defense position in a few sentences. If they cannot, the presentation probably delivered information without delivering a story.
