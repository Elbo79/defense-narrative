Defense visual strategy

How defense teams should think about countering plaintiff demonstratives.

The answer is rarely to make a defense version of every plaintiff graphic. Counter the idea that matters.

First ask what the visual is trying to make the audience believe

A timeline may imply inevitability. A medical illustration may imply mechanism. An animation may make a disputed sequence feel certain. A damages chart may turn assumptions into something that looks mathematically fixed. Identify the inference before deciding how to respond.

Decide whether the visual deserves a visual answer

Some demonstratives are best challenged through evidence, cross-examination, or admissibility arguments. Others create a mental model so powerful that the defense benefits from giving the audience a clearer competing model.

Expose what the plaintiff visual leaves out

A strong counter-demonstrative can add missing chronology, alternative causes, omitted records, scale, context, assumptions, or competing testimony. The goal is not visual symmetry; it is comprehension.

Use simplicity as an advantage

Defense presentations often have more qualifications and more records. That makes discipline especially important. One well-designed comparison that changes how an audience understands causation can be more useful than twenty slides of rebuttal.
